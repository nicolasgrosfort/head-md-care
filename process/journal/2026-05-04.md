# Key decisions

- **Two types of care in tension:** This becomes a structural axis of the project.
  1. **Nature care (non-action):** Letting the cycle proceed. The leaf falls because it must. Do not interrupt.
  2. **Human care (action):** Tending, ordering, making the space habitable for the living. Hans rakes, weeds, cleans.
  - Neither is wrong. Both are genuine forms of care. The tension is authentic.

# Key ideas

- **Care as indispensable to human habitability, not to nature:** Watching Cynthia Fleury's lecture. Wild nature is autonomous and resilient — it does not need human care. Only domesticated nature (parks, cemeteries) requires maintenance. Therefore: the gardener's care is actually care *for humans*, not for nature itself.
- **Mummy as resistance to death:** Thanatopraxis and embalming as a form of refusal to accept biological death — refusal to enter the cycle.
- **Story experiments:** Hans picks up a leaf → enters another dimension where time slows. What if the player *becomes* the leaf? Or *becomes* nature itself (wind, rain, sun)? Two perspectives: embodying nature vs. embodying Hans. Early formulation of the action/non-action binary.
