# Key ideas

- **Life after death:** Reading the "Interroge" platform (Geneva library Q&A). Near-death experience accounts as a representation of the natural cycle — the dead leaf is not truly dead in nature's eyes. The cycle blurs the boundary between life and death.
- **Yew trees (ifs) as portals:** Sabrina confirms that yew trees were planted in cemeteries as symbolic passages to the realm of the dead. Yew is both lethal (berries, bark) and evergreen — simultaneously death and eternal life.
- **Reference: "L'Île des morts" (Böcklin, 1880):** Five paintings of a rocky island receiving the dead, arrived by boat. Ifs dominate the island. Resonates with the cemetery-as-threshold idea.
- **"Laisse les morts enterrer les morts" (Bible, Matthew 8:22):** A dead person is never truly dead as long as there is life to remember them.
- **Environmental storytelling:** Sabrina references Lovecraft's "At the Mountains of Madness" — story told through traces left behind, not through characters or dialogue. Ghost leaves? The yew as a center?

# Field observation

- Cemetery visit with Janka. Observed two female SEVE gardeners removing vines around a living tree with a cargo tricycle. The removed vines are placed on the ground, ready to die — nature subtracted from nature.
- Learned the cemetery was originally built for victims of the Black Plague; it became a Genevan Pantheon only 40 years ago.
