# Key ideas

- **Miyawaki forest method:** Discovered through Robin (arborist friend). Plant dense mixed native species to recreate wild autonomous forest conditions. Opposite of the cemetery gardener's work — a form of care that consists of stepping back and letting nature self-organize. Strong metaphor for wú wéi.
- **Cimetière des Rois as botanical garden:** Research reveals it was common to plant a tree next to each tomb, each with symbolic meaning. Examples:
  - Green oak (feuillage persistant) = eternal life
  - Weeping beech = grief
  - Cedar = victory of life
  - The Caran d'Ache pencil tree (*Calocedrus*)
  - The Spanish fir brought back from Spain in memory of a botanist's deceased wife
  - **Custom of planting trees next to tombs is no longer authorized** — a tension between historical practice and current regulations.
- **Cimetière des Rois as Genevan Pantheon:** It only became a Pantheon 40 years ago (Borges was buried there in 1986).

# Key decisions

- The cemetery functions as an open-air museum — this richness of arboreal meaning can be a narrative and visual resource.
