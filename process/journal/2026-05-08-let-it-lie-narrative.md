# Key decisions

- **Working title: *Let it lie*** — the phrase captures both the act (leaving the leaf where it is) and the philosophical stance (accepting death, letting things be).
- **Hans confirmed as protagonist:** A municipal cemetery gardener who tirelessly repeats daily tending tasks.
- **Pivot moment:** Hans holds a dead leaf in his palm → pauses → for the first time, contemplates death → chooses non-action.

# Key ideas

- **Feedback from Sabrina on narrative writing:** Leave more room for the reader's imagination. "Spring returns" is richer than "the sky clears" — it invites the reader to imagine what that spring looks like.
- **Final narrative version (Essai 5):**
  > Hans, gardener in a cemetery, tirelessly repeats his daily tending tasks — raking leaves, pulling weeds — without realizing that his care is quietly draining life. As autumn deepens into winter, darkness settles into Hans. One morning, a dead leaf in his palm, Hans pauses — and for the first time, contemplates death. He lets the leaf lie — chooses non-action, he is simply present. The dead leaf becomes a flower. The weed, a butterfly. Spring returns, and Hans, freed, fades away.
- **"Freed, fades away":** Hans's disappearance is liberation, not death — he releases his grip on the cycle and becomes part of it.
