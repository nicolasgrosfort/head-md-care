## Brief

### Exhibition

The goal of this workshop is to develop a tablet-based interactive story for the exhibition ***Bonjour, ça va ? Petites histoires pour créer du lien*** at the [Geneva Municipal Library](https://www.bge-geneve.ch) in October 2026.

Each Media Design team will present to the curator of the exhibition:
- a general concept in mid-May
- a final working prototype in mid-June

Depending on budgetary constraints and realistic production timelines, one or two of these prototypes will then be converted into a fully-produced iPad application.

*\*Hello, how are you? Short stories about building connections*

---

### Concept of Care

The original working title for the exhibition was « care », a popular word in the anglo-saxon theoretical world; this anglo-saxon definition of *care* will be our north star throughout this workshop.

The concept of care is multiple — taking care, caring for, caring about, … — but ultimately can be resolved down to the question of how to live amongst and for others, with intention. The term emerged in the 1980s from feminist theorists unsatisfied with traditional definitions of ethical behavior and their conceptions of the relationship of self and other.

Your goal will be to go out into the field, discover and record a community, a group, a non-profit, a person practicing a form of care, and translate these people's stories into a poetic interactive representation.

---

### Interactive Stories

Your final goal is to produce an interactive story representing one of the seven contexts pre-selected by the curatorial team, listed below. This list is large and covers many aspects of living together, caring for others, and creating inclusive alliances and community. All seven of the contexts take place in Geneva.

This story can be told through interactive dialogues, spatial storytelling, even gameplay mechanics: visual puzzles, walking simulators, choose-your-own-adventures, non-linear interviews, interactive documentaries, collection games, customization games, farming/garden simulators, et cætera.

---

### Less Is More

However you choose to tell these stories, there must be a simple, core poetic resonance between the mechanics of the interaction and the story of your chosen context. A narratology class will be given near the beginning of the workshop to help you focus your stories to this core poetic proposition.

---

### Story Shape

This brief does not impose any one specific shape or form for your interactive story. One of your goals is, precisely, to find your own unique poetic angle that will tell — using simple and elegant means — the story of a community / context / group.

That said, perhaps as a guiding light to help you understand what we mean by poetic experience, take a look at the powerful non-linear visual novel *Building Stories* by Chris Ware, 2012.

---

### Narrative as Agency

> « We are autonomous when we feel … the power to gather our own life into an intelligible and acceptable narrative; to be able to tell a story, to reclaim a narrative. We are autonomous when we are able to tell our own story, as the agent of a story — our own.
>
> On est autonome quand on ressent … le pouvoir de rassembler sa propre vie dans un récit intelligible et acceptable ; être capable de faire récit, se réapproprier un récit. On est autonome quand on est capable de se raconter comme agent d'une histoire — la sienne. »
>
> – Cynthia Fleury, [Éthique du care](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=...) (YouTube, cf. 12:00)

---

### Collection

One of the goals of this workshop is to learn how to collect stories from a community, specifically: how to listen, observe, and collect the stories of others. During this collection phase, you will use whichever of the multiple means you wish: notes, drawings, sound and visual recordings, motion capture, body-scans, site-specific scans, 3D modelling, etc. There are many ways to capture the stories of others.

Take your time, with people, with yourselves. Go back, make a second, third, fourth appointment. Re-record. Show your drawings, show your 3D, show your cardboard prototypes, play with your subjects, get feedback from your contacts.

Once you have found a respectful and collaborative means to collect stories, you will translate these stories into your own poetic interactive representation.

---

### Inclusive Design

Many of the contexts identified by the curatorial team imply a politics of inclusion. Many of the core concepts and tips related to inclusive design, transfer to telling stories of inclusivity. The strongest introduction to date is Kat Holmes' work for *Mismatch: How Inclusion Shapes Design*. We will use several insights from this book to guide us in our process.

---

### Oneself As Another

While all seven contexts are collective in nature, you might end up choosing to tell the story of one specific individual. If this turns out to be your story approach, this individual should nevertheless act as a portal into their larger social web.

---

### Integrating Skills

In this workshop you will learn to integrate the many methods and technologies you have already explored throughout this first year of the Masters programme, including the technologies you have recently been learning in 3D modelling, scanning, and rigging. One goal of this workshop is to take all this knowledge and use it to build a fully integrated experience.

---

### Tablet Stories

All projects will be iPad tablet-based, which is the final form chosen for our participation in the exhibition.

---

### Teams

- 5 × team of 3 students
- 1 × team of 2 students

Each team should have one person comfortable with speaking in French.

### Technology

Complementing your heart and soul, you will also be using the game engine Unity — a game engine used to develop the majority of mobile games. This tool will force you to understand framerates, polygon count, and generally keeping things simple with computational resources.

Inside of your Unity-based interactive story, you will leverage the 3D tools you have just learned in the two previous workshops: 3D Modelling, 3D Scanning, Rigging, Motion Capture, Animation, et cætera. Stories will be based in 3D storytelling.

Tutorials: [head-md-care-unity](https://bit.ly/head-md-care-unity) — please feel free to add new code that might be useful for others.

---

### Audio

We have prepared Zoom audio recorders for you to take with you into the field for your recordings. We have reserved the motion capture suit from the Digital Pool that you can use to directly capture the body movements of your subjects.

### Podcast

A series of three podcasts is currently in development by the journalist Karine Pollien for the exhibition: Les Sureaux, Espace quartier des augustins, and Les maisons collectives. Students are welcome to integrate — in whole or in part — these podcasts into their interactive stories.

### Talking to Robots

We will be leveraging the insane evolution in the past few months of vibe coding, code completion, and coding chatbots to give you easier access to development with Unity, VS Code, and the C# programming language.

---

### Coach

AI is currently being sold as a replacement for human labor, but one alternative is to invert this relationship and use it as your advisor: for both the technical and production aspects of your project. In this project you will leverage the latest context engineering techniques to crash test your ideas and create your production plan. Let the robot help you with the complicated bits.

## Projects

### Working Title

Create a working title for each story. This can change.

### Student Names

First LAST, First LAST, First LAST

### Cardboard Prototype

Make a simple cardboard prototype of your story world. We will present these to Muriel & Cassandre.

### Illustration / Photo

Make a drawing and/or take a picture of your cardboard prototype and add on your project page below.

### Intention

Describe your intention in three sentences. Weave into your intention three sentences that include all of the following aspects:

- **Context:** what is the context you are covering? Focus on what is important, ignore all the little details.
- **Persona(s):** there is no single way to describe a persona, but you need to situate them inside of their world/context. Think about why we (as readers/players) will care about their story. Ask yourself what do they want, what do they need, what are their struggles?
- **Plot:** Try to define a central dilemma that does not need to be resolved, but does need to be clearly defined, in the story. This dilemma is what their story is about. This dilemma must imply a central question of care.
