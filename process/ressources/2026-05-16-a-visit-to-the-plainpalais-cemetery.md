# Notes

## Introduction

The origin of the name ==Plainpalais== may come from the ==marshy plain (plana palus in Latin)== that lay near the course of the Arve river. Around the year 1000, as the writer Henri Friderich (1874–1939) recalls, the river "formed numerous marshes in its unstable course" (cf. Plainpalais, notice historique, Geneva, Sonor, 1924, p. 9).

p.13

==Humans weary of life took advantage of this isolated spot and the abundant flow of the watercourses to end their days==. The first bodies buried in this extra muros cemetery were those of epidemic victims. Indeed, a contagious disease was recorded, which the Genevans nicknamed "==the [Black Plague](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peste_noire)==".

p.14

==These patients could find "no place to lie down", they were "rejected from hospitals", died "on the public road, without care, without the administration of sacraments". The abandoned corpses contributed to the spread of the disease "which had been raging for about thirty years in the city."== The syndics and citizens "wishing to remedy such a great calamity" had begun "to ==erect a hospital outside the city walls, in a place sufficiently remote and adapted to receive the aforementioned persons, with a cemetery in order to bury the bodies of the people who died there."==


> [!NOTE] Hospital and Chapel
> That is why there was a hospital there!

==The hospital and the church were definitively razed in 1776.== The cemetery remained the property of the Hôpital général until 27 February 1869 and was ceded from that date to the City of Geneva. The last two chapels were demolished in 1862.
