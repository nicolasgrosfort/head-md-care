## Autonomy and vulnerability — ==Paul Ricoeur==

Autonomy as a very strong interdependence with the other.

Freedom and autonomy pass through consent to the law (link to Japan?) that we give ourselves.

Autonomy as attestation of a possible self. Vulnerability as difficulty in attesting that self.

Autonomy is inseparable from a capacity to speak and a capacity to act.

==The power to gather one's own life into an intelligible and acceptable narrative.== To be capable of narrating oneself, of reclaiming a narrative. ==One is autonomous when one is capable of narrating oneself as the agent of one's own story.==

To be autonomous is to be identified as a subject — as a non-alienated being.

The other dimension of autonomy is ==imputability== — the capacity to be accountable. The full range of means deployed to produce a response.

Autonomy as trust in one's own capacity to… perceive oneself as a capable being, and to be recognised by others as capable too. → Logic of peer recognition. Approval by others.

To elicit the possibility of a sense of capability.

Autonomy as the capacity to speak and to act. The capacity to speak as the foundation for equal opportunity. Passage to action through language.

The vulnerable are often dispossessed of the performativity of doing and saying. A double penalty.

One is vulnerable if one believes in one's own vulnerability.

The awareness I bring to my power to act is itself part of that power. To believe I can is already to be capable.

Narrative identity. Autonomy allows it to be shaped, to construct a narrative. To establish a narrative coherence. To connect events, elements — and possibly catastrophes.

The binary of vulnerability/autonomy does not exist. We are all both, simultaneously, at every moment. ==The autonomous person is one who is capable of constructing a narrative from within that vulnerability.==

Dialectic between ipseity (self, alterity) and sameness (idem). Identity with oneself is not sameness. Identity refers to a history, therefore to a series of events and changes. The subject constructs a story from this singularity, taking vulnerability into account as a narrative moment of the subject, not as an end in itself.

==Autonomy is built through its creative interweaving with vulnerability.==

**Care**: ==a characteristic activity not of women but of the human species, with the goal of maintaining, perpetuating and repairing our world so that we can live in it as well as possible. Bodies, persons and environment included — which we seek to weave into a complex network in support of life.==

In Winnicott: the mother, through her care, makes the world habitable. Primary maternal preoccupation.

==Care is what repairs the world, but it is also what allows us to inhabit the world — in the sense that it is what makes the world habitable.== Because from the outset it is not habitable.

"==The world is only habitable because of care.=="

> [!Warning] Important
> Central concept: the ethics of care makes the world habitable.

[Epiméleia](https://hal.science/halshs-01886879) — Aristotle — ==The Greek term epimeleia most adequately corresponds to what we mean by care in its most general sense, whether that care applies to something or to someone.==

The notion of the **citizen** in Aristotle: defined by being governed and governing. The same person can be in both positions.

Care: beyond the idea of putting oneself in the other's place — but the capacity to put oneself in the other's place in the sense of their moral conception of the world.

This capacity to see the world from the other's point of view is a critical virtue for the exercise of care.

==Care is not a natural disposition, but a cultural one.==

**4 aspects of care, according to Tronto:**
1. **Caring about** — noticing the existence of a need and assessing the possibility of responding to it.
   Implies an engagement of perception: noticing + practical intelligence: assessing the possibility of responding to that noticing. A disposition of attention.
2. **Taking care of** — assuming responsibility. The full range of means deployed to produce a response.
3. **Care giving** — the singularity within care — not reducing care to merely a gift. It is also an activity. An economy. A skill. Professionalising, economic, social. Skills are in play.
4. **Care receiving** — recognising how the one who receives care responds to it. Evaluation of the entire care process. We need the other's reaction. A dialectic of reciprocity. Allows the process to be evaluated.

Link with **Yves Citton** ([ecology of attention](https://www.payot.ch/Detail/9782757884164)): our attention is constantly captured by new technologies. ==How can we establish attention-preserving ecologies?==

Attention as a decision of moral resolution. I decide to pay attention to the other.

==Care as a means of restoring to others the sense of their own dignity, and therefore restoring what some might call self-esteem.==

Each of us passes simultaneously through varying degrees of dependence and independence.

Care as a three-entry system: autonomy, vulnerability and reciprocity.

No dichotomy between autonomous and vulnerable, but a difference between dependent and dependent. Some manage to make this dependence invisible, while others cannot.

→ A form of irresponsibility among elites/the privileged.

Elites have providers of care whom they render invisible, and in doing so prevent those providers from attending to their own care.

→ Not entirely clear…

==The world is not liveable. It is only liveable because of care.==

==Effacing oneself is also part of care.== "And that is where it gets complicated" → helping without the other feeling entirely that they are being helped. A dialectic between self-effacement and a self-effacement that is far from simple.

The universality of care — an essential dimension of anthropology.

==There are no dependents and independents, only the interdependent — who recognise this interdependence more or less, and who have the means or not to deny and render invisible this interdependence.==

**Autonomy**: delegating part of one's care work to someone other than oneself. Passing on the hot potato of one's own care to someone else. → A shared dependence on care provided by another. Requires skills so as not to threaten the integrity of the self.

How to free society from its denial of structural dependencies? → Vulnerability as the centre of gravity of our society. We are all dependent.

A distinction between those who can procure the care they need for themselves, and those who cannot. ==But everyone needs care.==

Care — between tact and tactics.

To what extent do we act and decide for others?

Autonomy does not exist in the Kantian sense. Because the human being is by hypothesis autonomous, they must become it. The principle of autonomy belongs to fiction, to narrative identity, to narrative. It is essential for founding and organising the aim of emancipation. To believe oneself capable is already to enter the sphere of autonomy.
