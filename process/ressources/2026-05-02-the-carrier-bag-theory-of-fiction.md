# Notes

In the Palaeolithic, Neolithic and prehistoric era, between 65 and 80% of ==what human beings ate in those regions was gathered==; meat constituted the main diet only in the extreme Arctic.

And we did not even need to work hard for it — much less hard, in any case, than enslaved peasants in another's field since the invention of agriculture, ==much less than wage workers since the invention of civilisation==. An average prehistoric human could live well by working about fifteen hours a week.

Skilled hunters could therefore return staggering under a burden of meat, arms full of ivory, and with a story. ==But it was not the meat that made the difference. It was the story.==

That story contains not only Action, ==it has a Hero. And Heroes are powerful.==

And the _hero_, in Woolf's dictionary, becomes a "bottle." The hero as bottle — already a severe revaluation. ==I now propose to consider the bottle as a hero.==

==If you have nothing to put it in, food will escape you== — even something as uncombative and undexterous as oats.


> [!NOTE] 
> The hero as the conceptual container of a narrative? Purely utilitarian?

A leaf a gourd a shell a net a bag a shawl a sack a pot a box a container. ==A receptacle. A vessel.==

"==The first cultural equipment was probably a container.== (…) Many theorists feel that the first cultural inventions must have been on the one hand a container to hold gathered foodstuffs, and on the other hand some kind of sling or net carrier."

*Elizabeth Fisher in Women's Creation*

==We have heard that story, we have all heard about sticks and spears and swords==, all those things you hit, thrust, and bang with, all the long, hard things. ==But we have heard nothing about the thing you put things in==, about the container and what it contains. Now there is a new story. There is one.

...==for what is the use of digging up a lot of potatoes if you have nothing to bring home those you cannot eat on the spot==; at the same time as — or before — the tool that channels energy outward, we made the tool that brings energy home.

I disagree with all of this. ==I would go so far as to say that the natural, proper, fitting shape of the novel might be that of a bag, a sack.== ==A book holds words. Words hold things. They carry meanings.== A novel is a medicine bundle, holding things in a particular, powerful relation to one another.

That is why I like novels: ==instead of heroes, they contain people.==

If, however, one avoids the linear, progressive, Techno-Heroic mode, embracing the (lethal) arrow of Time, ==if one redefines technology and science as the primary cultural carrier bag rather than as a weapon of domination, then we will have, as a rather pleasant side effect, the possibility of seeing science fiction as a far less rigid and narrow field==, not necessarily Promethean or apocalyptic, and as a genre in fact less mythological than realistic.
