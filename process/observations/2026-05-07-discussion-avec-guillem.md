# Context

- **With:** Guillem — landscape architect, studied cemetery design as part of his master's project.
- **Trigger:** Follow-up after reading *Renouveler le paysage funéraire*.

# Key observations

- **Cologny cemetery:** all gravel, severe heat island effect — a problematic design that illustrates how "green cemetery" is often misunderstood.
- **Dead soils:** burying at 6 feet works bacterially in theory, but Geneva's soils are too clay-like and anaerobic — bodies don't decompose. Non-decomposed bodies found when reopening tombs.
- **Cremation** seen as a more viable solution: mix ashes with soil to create compost (carbon, a base of life). Also enables multiple recueillement sites.
- **Miyawaki forests:** too anthropocentric and labor-intensive — must be watered and tended constantly. Not a convincing direction.
- **Friche (wasteland):** the closest thing to an explosion of life. You start from environment A; life develops despite constraints. Non-human and human alike. No maintenance.
- **Tiers lieux:** undefined spaces (old parking lots, industrial wasteland) where the most life happens — cultural, social, and biological biodiversity. Places that escape definition, where children build mental maps, where animals hunt undisturbed.
- **Yews at Cimetière des Rois:** removing even a few causes grief in the public — they destroy imaginaries, narratives, representations. Yet yews create monoculture and damage soils and sidewalks.

# Key ideas

- **Compartmentalizing death mirrors how we treat the planet:** enclosing the dead, breaking cycles, destroying biodiversity. The gravel cemetery as ecological symptom.
- **Jardin des souvenirs:** collective memory space for mourning. Can exist anywhere in the city — not tied to a burial site. Full imaginaire, no fixed location. The most interesting direction.
- **Not a place dedicated to the dead — a place dedicated to life.**
- **Día de Muertos** and other alternative cultural approaches to death worth exploring: the problem is societal, about where we place the dead in our cities.
- **The undetermined is where the most life lives** — friche, tiers lieux, spaces that escape human control.
- How spatial design could reconfigure our imaginaries — and therefore our relationship to death.

# Open questions

- Why are the dead enclosed in cemeteries?
- How could a landscape gesture simultaneously care for the natural, the built, and the human?
- What is a "green cemetery" actually?
