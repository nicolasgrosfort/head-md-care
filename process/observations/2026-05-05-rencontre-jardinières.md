# Context

- **With:** Two agents from SEVE (Service des Espaces Verts et de l'Environnement), Cimetière des Rois.
- **Format:** Informal interview on site.

# Key observations

## Work context

- Cemetery described as **a particularly pleasant workplace:** calm, few visitors, no dogs (dogs forbidden).
- Two agents at **~60% each**, organized so someone is always on site.
- **No reference to death** in their daily work — they feel like they work in a park. The question of death did not emerge spontaneously.

## Tree maintenance

- Currently removing **ivy from a rare cedar** to reveal and preserve the bark — both preservation (ivy can damage bark) and aesthetic (showcasing texture and quality).
- Role split: **SEVE** handles soil, mowing, and tomb surroundings. **Arborists** handle the trees themselves (pruning, care).

## Tomb maintenance

- Tombs maintained **by families** when relatives exist; **by the commune/state** when there are no direct heirs.
- SEVE participates in cleaning and vegetation management around tombstones.

## Symbolism of yews (ifs)

- **Yews (Taxus)** are traditionally planted in cemeteries as symbolic **gateways to the beyond** — a culturally charged presence that reinforces the significance of the cemetery's remarkable trees.

## Anecdotes

- **Ghost stories** reported by some visitors. The agents don't take them seriously, but the stories contribute to the cemetery's imaginary.

# Key ideas

- **Cemetery workers don't experience death at work** — it's a park to them. The death is invisible in the daily practice.
- **Ivy removal as care:** preserving the tree, revealing its beauty — an act both ecological and aesthetic.
- **Yews as symbolic gateways:** the cemetery's trees carry cultural and spiritual charge beyond their physical presence.
