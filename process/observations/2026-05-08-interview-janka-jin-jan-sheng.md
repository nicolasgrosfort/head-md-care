# Context

- **With:** Jin Jan Sheng — cemetery owner, interviewed by Janka.
- **Language:** English.

# Key observations

- **Cemetery as a space where time has stopped:** slow, quiet, nobody comes to disturb it.
- **Visitors by age:**
  - Elderly: calm, flowers, peaceful mood.
  - Middle-aged: Qingming Festival, offerings, blessings for the family.
  - Young children: treat it like a park — curious, don't yet understand death.
  - Young adults: come rarely.
- **The devoted widower:** one man came almost every day in the first year after his wife died. Her grave always has the freshest flowers.
  - His motivation: **fear** — afraid of returning to normal life, afraid of accepting absence.
- **Flowers attract small animals** as a food source — removed before they rot. An invisible form of maintenance labor.
- **After 20 years:** emotional reaction to death has become less immediate. Witnessing many unexpected and tragic deaths led to valuing life more deeply.
- **On tombstones:** they don't record whether someone was successful or not — only a name. After death, nobody cares about wealth or personal gain.
- **On relationships:** *"Everyone is only a passerby in your life. No relationship can stay with you forever. When certain connections begin to fade away, you should learn to face it openly."*

# Key ideas

- **Fear as a driver of grief rituals:** staying close to the dead as a refusal to accept absence.
- **Normalization of death through prolonged exposure** can deepen appreciation for life — not desensitization, but reorientation.
- **The cemetery as a scale restorer:** human relationships and ambitions seen in their true proportions.
